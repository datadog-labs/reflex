Clients submit jobs to a shared queue. The scheduler places each job on a node with enough CPU and memory.Read moreShow less

Adjust each client’s arrival rate, job size, duration, and priority. Watch how resource usage and waiting times change as jobs compete for capacity.

Jobs move through four states:

  • Queued: Waiting for placement.
  • Running: Consuming CPU and memory on a node.
  • Completed: Finished and released its resources.
  • Rejected: The queue is full, or the job exceeds every node’s capacity.

Jev recommends which job to place on which node, or whether to defer. It considers available resources, waiting times, and client priorities. Running jobs continue to completion.

Reflex checks each placement: the job must still be queued, be the oldest request from its client, and fit the node’s available capacity. Recommendations expire after five simulated seconds and are rejected if priorities change. Once eligible jobs have waited 30 seconds, the oldest one that fits must be scheduled next.

Capacity is a promise.

Each client submits jobs at its configured rate, with fixed CPU, memory, and expected duration. Actual durations vary deterministically by ±20% using the run seed. Jev sees estimates only.

Each client has a FIFO queue. Jev chooses among the oldest fitting requests from different clients, favoring Critical over High over Normal. A blocked head holds only its own client queue. After 30 seconds, the oldest fitting client head must be served before younger heads; this is an eligibility rule, not a guaranteed waiting-time bound. CPU and memory are reserved together through the Reflex executor. Jobs run to completion without preemption.

Click a client to change its workload, a node to inspect running jobs, or the shared queue to inspect waiting jobs. Moving dots follow actual arrivals and accepted placements; animation is sampled for readability. Separate bars show reserved CPU and memory. Colored job blocks identify clients and show running work. Finished jobs leave their node and release their reservations.

Jev chooses a request and Node A, B, C, or D from feasible placements, or Defer when the aging rule permits it. Guards recheck client FIFO, priority revisions, aging, capacity, and evidence age (at most 5 simulated seconds). Live priority edits cancel pending recommendations and affect queued and future requests immediately, without restarting the run. Defer leaves all queues unchanged. API failures leave it queued and are shown in the journal.

At most one inference runs at a time, with at least 200 ms between dispatches in the web playground. Evaluations continue until the simulation ends. This caps placement throughput in Jev mode; arrivals and completions continue during inference. Baselines act immediately. These modes illustrate behavior, not a controlled inference-latency benchmark.

First Fit and Best Fit first select the highest-priority fitting client head, breaking ties by arrival order, subject to the same aging rule. First Fit then chooses the first fitting node; Best Fit minimizes the sum of remaining CPU and memory fractions. Both use the same guards. Switching policy resets the run while keeping client settings and cumulative cost.

One to eight clients are supported. Pause a client or set its rate to zero to stop new arrivals. Removing a client leaves its existing jobs intact. Jobs larger than every node or arriving to a full 128-job queue are rejected. Local runs stop after three simulated minutes; Datadog runs allow ten minutes, possibly with unfinished jobs.

When Datadog evidence is enabled, node reservations and queue pressure come from delayed Datadog measurements. The scheduler supplies the exact candidate and legal choices, and Reflex rechecks capacity before placement. The evidence inspector identifies the source and observation timestamps. This mode requires continuous 1× playback; pause cancels pending work and resume warms up fresh telemetry.
